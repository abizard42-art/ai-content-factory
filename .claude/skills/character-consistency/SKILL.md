# Character Consistency — Raka

## Canonical design
Raka is a single recurring 2D anime-style male host:
- youthful appearance;
- warm medium-tan skin;
- large brown eyes;
- thick, tousled black hair;
- friendly, expressive face and open smile;
- tan-brown explorer jacket over a black shirt;
- compass pendant necklace.

The saved reference image of Raka in front of the Giza pyramids is the canonical visual benchmark. When the image tool or workflow supports reference images, use that same approved reference rather than inventing a new design.

## Invariants
Keep these stable across scenes:
- face shape and apparent age;
- skin tone and eye color;
- hairstyle and hair color;
- jacket color and overall outfit;
- black inner shirt;
- compass pendant;
- overall 2D anime illustration style.

## Allowed variation
- expression, pose, hand gesture, and camera angle;
- lighting and environment;
- small context-appropriate accessories only when explicitly requested;
- clothing may change only when the user approves a deliberate costume variation.

## Scene composition
- Raka should appear only when he adds explanatory or emotional value.
- Do not force him into every scene.
- For scenes without Raka, focus on the historical subject, map, artefact, diagram, or environment.
- When Raka appears, do not let props obscure his face or signature pendant without a clear reason.

## Review checklist
Before accepting a generated image, compare it with the canonical reference:
1. Does the face still read as the same character?
2. Are hair, skin tone, eyes, jacket, shirt, and pendant consistent?
3. Is the image still in the approved 2D style?
4. Does the pose fit the scene without changing the character design?
5. Is the historical setting accurate and free of anachronistic objects?

If a mismatch is significant, regenerate or flag for human review. Never silently redefine the canonical character.
